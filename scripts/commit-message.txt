Trust repair: four factual defects on pavilion profiles, and the National Day claim

Step one of the Expo content plan, and its precondition. An external review pass
found that several pavilion profiles contradict themselves: `facts` rows were
updated when news landed, while the hand-written prose and FAQs on the same page
were not. One profile also carried a wrong attribution. Publishing new visitor
content on top of a record that disagrees with itself would only multiply the
correction surface, so this lands first.

- **Slovakia.** Its prose and FAQ said the winning design "isn't public yet,"
  while its own facts already named EXPO LINE's "Living Playground", announced
  9 July 2026 with a €375,000 design-services contract. Prose and FAQ now name it.

- **Türkiye.** Its prose and FAQ said size had not been announced, while its own
  summary and facts gave the 648 m² plot (27 × 24 m) on parcel A5.1, due ready
  for operation by 15 April 2027. Both now state what is known and confine the
  "not announced" to design and budget, where it is still true.

- **Austria.** Its prose and one FAQ said the EU-wide two-stage tender "is under
  way" while another FAQ named "Join the Flow" as its 4 August 2026 winner — the
  same page asserting both. In **both languages**. Fixed in all four places.

- **Switzerland.** The profile described the pavilion as a walk-through mountain
  panorama "engineered by Werner Sobek AG with Danilo Dangubic Architects and
  VOGT". Checking the cited source showed those firms are engaged on the **wider
  Expo site** — the Best Practice Zone timber pavilions, the shading structure
  and the landscape — not on the Swiss national pavilion. This was a wrong
  attribution, not merely a stale one. It is removed, the Design fact is
  rewritten to what the persoenlich.com source actually supports (648 m² inside
  a shared hall, scenography by Jeff), and the page carries a dated public
  correction note rather than quietly dropping the claim.

Separately, `/expo-2027/programme` told readers that "you cannot yet pick a date
because your country falls on it." That is no longer true: Türkiye's Ministry of
Trade names **19 May 2027** as its planned Expo National Day. Corrected in EN and
DE, in both the body and the FAQ, along with two pieces of context worth having:
19 May is also a Turkish public holiday (Commemoration of Atatürk, Youth and
Sports Day), though nothing published explains whether that drove the choice; and
at Expo 2025 Osaka the organiser released National Day dates in batches, with 131
of 161 participants published roughly ten months before opening. Readers are now
told their own country's Expo office is often ahead of the organiser.

The date is labelled **planned**, not confirmed — the ministry notice says so.
