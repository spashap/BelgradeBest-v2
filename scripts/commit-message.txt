Reconcile the Expo visitor forecast across EN + DE; drop two over-inferences

Our own participants tracker has recorded since 20 August that the BIE now
states "more than 6 million" visits and that the six-million figure is the more
recent. Every visitor-facing Expo article, meanwhile, still presented the
Participants FAQ's 4.1 million model as settled — roughly twenty statements
across five English articles and their five German twins. The site was
contradicting itself on its single most-quoted number.

Forecasts are now presented chronologically wherever they appear: earlier
official statements of 2.6–3 million, the Participants FAQ's ~4.1 million, the
organiser's own later argument that 6 million is realistic, and the BIE's
current "more than 6 million" — all labelled projections rather than counts, and
all noting that most sources count visits, not unique visitors, so a three-day
ticket is three visits.

The per-day numbers needed the same treatment. The much-repeated 44,652 daily
average and 89,304 peak are derived from the 4.1-million model, not the
six-million one; on the same 93 days six million averages nearer 64,500 a day,
and no updated per-day model has been published alongside the higher figure.
Every page that quotes those figures now says so.

Two over-inferences went with it:

- **Tickets.** "Most admissions will almost certainly be digital" was doing more
  work than the evidence allows. 200,000 of the 1.2 million printed tickets are
  three-day tickets, so the printed stock covers more admissions than it has
  pieces, and nothing stops the organiser printing more. The page now says a
  substantial digital channel is reasonable to expect, and that a digital
  majority is not established.
- **Accommodation.** Dividing Expo visits by hotel rooms is not a shortage
  calculation: visits include Belgrade residents, regional day-trippers and the
  same person returning on a three-day ticket. Nobody has published how many
  visitors need a bed, for how many nights. The page now states the direction of
  travel — a fixed room stock against a large and rising projection — as the
  reason to book early, and explicitly declines to predict a specific shortfall.
  The related "three times Belgrade's annual tourist volume" comparison keeps its
  attribution but gains the caveat that visits and tourist arrivals are not the
  same unit.

Also corrected: the 8,000-events figure is published by the BIE itself, so
describing it as "a non-official consolidation" was wrong. It is now attributed,
and still flagged as planned scale rather than a confirmed count since no dated
programme backs it up.
