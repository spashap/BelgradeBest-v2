Structural pass: main landmark, un-paginate the TOC, answer-first practical pages

Completes items 5–9 of the content-audit workplan
(BELGRADEBEST_CONTENT_AUDIT.md §7). Everything here came out of the same
measurement: the short answer arrived first, but the section that actually does
the work sat behind a decorative image and a contents list that hid its own
later entries.

**A main landmark, at last.** Articles had none — `<header>`, four `<nav>`s,
`<article>`, `<footer>`, and no `<main>` anywhere. A keyboard or screen-reader
user had to tab the whole header on every page, and content extractors had no
main-content signal, which matters now that AI assistants are a live channel.
BaseLayout renders one `<main id="main">` for every public page plus a localised
skip link ("Zum Inhalt springen" on German pages); index.astro drops the
duplicate it was supplying itself. Footer navigations get `aria-labelledby`
pointing at their own visible headings, so assistive tech can tell "Explore"
from "The project" instead of announcing three unlabelled navigations. The new
`.c-skiplink` pattern went into globals.css before it was used, per the Design
Law.

**The table of contents stops hiding sections.** It paginated into horizontal
slides of ten behind prev/next arrows — so on an 18-section article, eight
sections were invisible in the very list whose job is to reveal them. Eleven
articles were affected, and restoring the lost content last commit made it
worse. All items now render in one two-column list; the inline slider script is
gone.

**Practical pages lead with the answer.** On /plan-your-trip the reader has a
job — get in from the airport, pay for something, check a visa — and a decorative
hero sat between the short answer and the detail. Measured before: lede, image at
~4.4KB, TOC at ~5.0KB, first section at ~6.2KB. After: **first section at 1.8KB.**
The hero is still generated and still used as the OG/social card through
`meta.image`; it just no longer delays the answer. Leisure legs keep theirs,
where browsing is part of the point.

**Links where the decision is made, not only at the bottom.** Three contextual
links, each giving a reason rather than a destination name: how to get dinars,
beside the A1 fare that must be paid in cash; connectivity, beside the ride-hail
option that silently requires data on arrival; airport transfer costs, beside the
warning about airport exchange rates. This copies a pattern the taxi article
already used well.

**The homepage answers before it advertises.** New order: identity → "Start here
— the questions everyone asks first" (seven task routes, each a one-line answer
and a link to the real guide) → browse all guides → a compact Expo feature →
credibility. Expo was previously the first thing every visitor met, for an event
eight months out, on a page whose arrivals are overwhelmingly not searching for
it. The separate FAQ section is cut: it duplicated the task block above it, and
cutting it also removes its JSON-LD, since that is now gated on the same flag.

**Two reviewed articles lose their FAQ frontmatter.** All 15 questions on
airport-to-city and money were already answered in their bodies — which is the
evidence for the earlier finding that literal heading mismatch badly overstated
how much of the hidden FAQ backlog was novel. Deleting them also takes the
contradictory airport-rail date with it, so the two copies can no longer
disagree.

Verified in dist: exactly one `<main>` and a skip link on every page type, zero
TOC pagination anywhere, practical first-section at byte 1758, the decision-point
links present, homepage order as above, no FAQPage markup on the homepage.
Sitemap unchanged at 141. Build green with the content integrity gate active.

growth/growth_changes.jsonl gains BB-CHG-0012. Item 11 (a monthly 20-minute
review) is a habit, not a commit; everything else on the audit workplan is done.
