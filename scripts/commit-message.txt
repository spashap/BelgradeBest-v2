Restore 84 lost article sections, gate the build, withdraw medical tourism

A content audit asking "does the visitor get what they came for" found that the
answer had been no for three months, and nobody knew.

## 17 articles were incomplete since 22 June

Commit e928564 ("Build /glossary knowledge-pages spoke set") silently dropped
whole `##` sections from 17 articles. Repair passes on 2 July and 7 August fixed
only the ones that ended mid-sentence. **The rest ended on a clean full stop and
passed every scan.**

/visit-belgrade/things-to-do-in-belgrade was serving 4 of its 10 attractions —
no Skadarlija, no Zemun, no Ada Ciganlija, no Avala Tower. best-restaurants had
lost 55% of its body, splavovi 51%, kafanas 49%, craft-beer 48%.

scripts/restore-lost-sections.mjs re-inserts the missing sections from revision
9645a97 in their original positions, leaving every section that still existed
untouched — so the July and August edits survive intact. **84 sections, roughly
15,000 words, across 16 articles**, plus two truncated tails and one leftover
heading stub repaired directly.

Eleven restored sections carry June-2026 prices or opening hours and are
UNVERIFIED. The script lists them; they need checking before they are trusted.

## The guard that would have caught it

src/lib/content-integrity.mjs runs at `astro:build:start` and **fails the build**.
Two independent checks:

1. **Damage** — ends without terminal punctuation, ends on a heading with no
   content, ends on a dangling function word, unclosed link.
2. **Shrinkage** — body more than 10% smaller than the size recorded in
   src/data/content-integrity.json.

The second check is the point. A punctuation check *cannot* prove completeness —
a section can vanish and leave a perfectly well-formed page, which is exactly why
three separate repair passes missed these. Verified against all three failure
modes, including silent section loss that ends cleanly.

Deliberate cuts stay easy, they just have to be stated:
`node scripts/update-content-baseline.mjs`, committed alongside the edit. That
turns shortening an article into a reviewable act instead of an accident.

It lives in src/lib rather than scripts/ on purpose — scripts/ is
.vercelignore'd, so a guard there would not run on deploys.

## Medical tourism withdrawn (15 articles + hub)

Two independent reasons. The owner's own research concluded Serbia is not
competitive against Hungary, Turkey or Moldova, so the commercial premise does
not hold. Separately, the content made treatment-relevant claims it could not
support: a 20–60% saving called "realistic" with no citation; surgical capacity
inferred from domestic waiting-list length; "ISO" listed as an accreditation when
ISO develops standards and does not issue certificates; an unevidenced ranking of
risks. A "not medical advice" line does not repair an unsupported claim that
influences a treatment decision.

Removed from public delivery rather than noindexed — a quarantined page still
serves the same guidance to anyone holding the link. The 15 articles are preserved
at KB/withdrawn/medical-tourism/ with a README recording the reasoning and what a
defensible minimum would look like. Leg removed from site-schema, header nav, the
LEGS enum, homepage ethos copy and the About disclaimer; the one cross-link from a
retained article cut; 60 orphaned hero images deleted. Zero dangling
/medical-tourism links remain in the build. Sitemap 157 → 141.

## Two claims the site could not back

ArticleLayout emitted FAQPage JSON-LD for 422 questions — roughly 27,000 words
across 64 English and 6 German articles — that the layout never renders. The
markup described content no reader could see, and the two copies had already
drifted: the airport article's FAQ gave a rail delivery date its own body
contradicted. Google retired FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026, so it bought nothing
either. Removed; the homepage had the same defect and its schema is now gated on
the same flag as its visible section. The frontmatter stays as editorial source
material to fold into bodies during page edits, not as a second maintained copy.

Every article also carried "Updated <date> · Source-checked by the BelgradeBest
editorial process" — attached automatically to any page with a date, with no
evidence of a review and no visible sources. Reduced to the date. The About page's
"every page is manually reviewed… nothing goes live unread" is replaced with an
accurate account, including this incident and the new guard, because a site that
publishes its own corrections is the kind of source we are trying to be.
