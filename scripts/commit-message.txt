Get the German Expo cluster crawled, found and searchable

Three connected fixes to the German pilot, which nine days after launch had
zero impressions in both engines.

1. CRAWLING — scripts/submit-to-bing.mjs (new, owner-run, not deployed)

Bing had never crawled a single one of the 11 German URLs. GetUrlInfo returned
the never-crawled sentinel (year 3939) for all of them while Bing was crawling
100+ English pages a day and holding 136 of 156 sitemap URLs. Google had indexed
1 of 11 and reported the hub as "URL is unknown to Google".

The cause was a gap we had documented but not closed: IndexNow cannot reach
Microsoft for this host — their endpoint 403s "UserForbiddedToAccessSite" (see
the comment in astro.config.mjs) — so build-time pings only ever land at
Yandex/Seznam/Naver and nothing has ever pushed a URL to Bing. That matters more
here than on most sites: Bing is this site's live search channel. Over the 28
days to 2026-09-11 it served ~1,340 impressions and 46 clicks against Google's
~300 impressions and 2 clicks over 60 days, and 84 of 92 GA4 organic sessions
came from the Bing index (bing + duckduckgo + ecosia + yahoo).

The script reads the LIVE sitemap, filters by --prefix, and submits through Bing
Webmaster's URL Submission API (free, 100/day). --verify reports per-URL crawl
status, --dry-run previews, --fresh skips already-crawled URLs. It paces itself
and retries with backoff around Bing's per-host throttle, which answers HTTP 400
+ ErrorCode 5 rather than 429; a URL whose lookup fails is reported UNKNOWN and
never treated as never-crawled, so --fresh errs toward skipping. Git Bash
rewrites a bare "/de/" into a Windows path, so the script detects that and
refuses rather than silently matching nothing — run it from PowerShell.

All 11 German URLs were submitted and accepted on 2026-09-13 (quota 100 -> 89).
A --all --fresh survey then showed the same gap beyond the German set: 2 /areas/
and 8 /glossary/ pages had never been crawled either, including /glossary/bie —
Expo vocabulary sitting uncrawled on the channel that actually ranks us. Those
were submitted in a follow-up run.

2. TARGETING — German titles and descriptions

The translation itself is strong (native Sie-form, localised dates, question-form
H2s, German FAQPage JSON-LD, 1,000-2,400 words a page), but three of six article
titles omitted "Belgrad" — including tickets, whose German query "expo belgrad
tickets" is the strongest German-intent term in our Bing data (9 impressions,
position 6.3). Every title now leads with "Expo 2027 Belgrad". "Weltausstellung"
and "Serbien" appeared in no German title or description at all; what-is-it now
names Weltausstellung as the contrast it genuinely is (this is a Specialised
Expo, not a World Expo) and guide names Serbien. The German hub's <title> drops
"der deutschsprachige Guide" — the page is already hreflang="de", so announcing
German-ness in the SERP bought nothing; the H1 keeps the editorial line.

All three DACH pavilion descriptions were truncating mid-sentence ("...der
Kategorie…"). Their i18n.de.summary fields are restructured into two sentences so
metaTrim yields a complete first sentence; listing `updated` bumped accordingly.

3. CROSS-LINKS — the new c-langnote pattern

The only English-to-German internal link was the two-letter "DE" header chip:
easy to miss, and worthless as anchor text. c-langnote (added to globals.css
first, per the Design Law, then used) is the in-content version, written in the
language it points AT — a German reader on an English page now reads a German
sentence, and the link carries German anchor text. It renders from the page's own
hreflang set, so it appears exactly where a twin exists and nowhere else.

All 11 German URLs now have a descriptive anchor-text link from their English
twin. The pavilion profiles name the German edition explicitly ("Deutscher
Pavillon: dieses Profil gibt es auch auf Deutsch"), since the DACH profiles are
the site's strongest German-intent magnet — /expo-2027/pavilions/germany already
pulls 11 Bing impressions and 4 clicks. The German pavilion atlas was left alone:
it already carries a German-language pointer to the English atlas at the bottom.

Verified in dist/: 11 c-langnote blocks pointing at German, zero on pages without
a twin, all six German titles carrying "Expo 2027 Belgrad", no truncated DACH
descriptions, hreflang/canonical/x-default untouched, sitemap unchanged at 156.
Build green.
