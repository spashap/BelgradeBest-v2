Homepage down to 270 words; no vote widget; close three integrity-gate bypasses

Two owner questions — is the homepage any good from a modern UI standpoint, and
should every article carry a thumbs up/down with results hidden. Answered over two
external review rounds, which also caught a defect I had shipped hours earlier.

## The defect first

The two quick-answer cards added this morning used a `body` field.
`GoodToKnow.astro` renders `text`. **Both shipped as a label with no answer** — in
a section whose intro promised "a straight answer to each." Exactly the class of
problem the whole audit was about: a page not honouring its own claim. Fixed.

## Homepage: shortened, not redesigned

The advice was to edit, not rebrand — at 46 landings per 60 days a visual refresh
would cost design, implementation and regression time that could not be
distinguished from normal variation.

- Six quick answers, not seven. "Where to stay" is cut: it has a nav entry and a
  category card immediately below it. Six also tiles cleanly; seven left one card
  orphaned on its own row.
- The eyebrow "Start here" appeared on two consecutive sections. Now
  **"Plan your first visit"** and **"Explore Belgrade"**.
- Both filler intros deleted — the cards demonstrate the promise without a
  sentence claiming it.
- **The standalone Expo feature is hidden.** Moving it down the page earlier had
  changed its order, not its weight: it was still a full-width 21:9 hero. Expo
  keeps its category card, nav entry and identity mention.
- The credibility block drops from a paragraph re-listing the site's own
  categories to one sentence and a link. Claiming to be honest is weaker than
  pointing at the evidence.

**515 → 270 words.** Deliberately kept: the narrow-intro / full-width-grid
convention (that is the `--readw` reading measure doing its job, not a stray
width), and the two card treatments — answer cards and browse cards are different
jobs and share one type system.

## The vote widget is not built, and that is the recommendation

At ~114 real sessions per 60 days, a 5% response rate is about **six
self-selected answers site-wide**. That can occasionally flag something worth a
look; it cannot rank articles, establish a usefulness rate, or show that a change
helped. Copying the listing-request storage pattern would also mean a GitHub
commit per vote, a rebuild trigger, no rate limiting and no deduplication — a lot
of machinery for an anonymous thumb.

The owner's instinct to hide results was right, and for a stronger reason than
he gave: "1 of 2 readers found this useful" is worse than silence.

What ships instead is the honest form of the same idea — a plain invitation on
three arrival-decision pages (airport-to-city, getting-around, 3-day itinerary):

> Something wrong, out of date, or missing from this guide? Tell us what you needed.

No endpoint, no scores, nothing public. One specific "you never said X" is
actionable on its own, which six votes are not.

**Reopen condition**, recorded so this is a deferral rather than a refusal: a
named article, a specific unresolved question, someone who will read the
responses, and plausible exposure for ~50 responses in 60 days.

## Three gate bypasses closed

Reviewing my own About wording turned up that the gate does less than I claimed
for it. A **missing baseline** made the shrinkage check silently vacuous — it
passed while checking nothing. An **emptied body** was skipped by a falsy guard.
An article **deleted from disk** was invisible. All three now fail the build,
each proven by reintroducing it.

The About sentence claiming the check "refuses to publish a page that has lost
content" was false and is narrowed to what it does: warn on damaged text and on
substantial drops in length, while not proving completeness. Writing an
over-claim into the sentence describing the fix for over-claiming is its own
small lesson.

elsewhere, About corrected. Build green. growth/growth_changes.jsonl gains
