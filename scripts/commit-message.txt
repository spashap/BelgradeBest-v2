Add /expo-2027/national-days — the National Day record, one master, no drift

Step two of BELGRADEBEST_EXPO_CONTENT_PLAN.md, and the first new asset.

A National Day date is the only fact on a pavilion profile that changes *which
day* a visitor travels. The organiser has not published the country-to-date
schedule — the calendar linked from its programme page is Playground Events —
but individual countries announce through their own ministries. That is the gap
this fills, and why it can exist before the organiser publishes.

It ships with **one dated entry**. That is deliberate, and the page says so in
its own voice: "we have recorded one dated announcement" is a statement about
this list, not about the world. Absence from it means not recorded by us, never
not announced. The same discipline runs through the entry itself — Türkiye's
19 May 2027 is labelled **planned**, because the Ministry of Trade notice says
planned, and each dated entry carries both what the announcement establishes and
what it does not. For Türkiye that second field is the honest bulk of it: times,
venue, performers, public access and reservations are all unestablished.

Two pieces of context earn their place:

- **An Expo National Day is not that country's national holiday**, and one
  cannot be derived from the other. Türkiye's 19 May happens to be both — it is
  Commemoration of Atatürk, Youth and Sports Day, which for a Sport-and-Music
  Expo is a striking fit — but Osaka disproves the rule: the Netherlands took
  21 May (King's Day is 27 April) and Sweden 14 May (National Day is 6 June).
- **How the dates are likely to arrive.** Osaka released them in batches; a June
  2024 release added 31 countries to an April batch, reaching 131 of 161 roughly
  ten months before opening. Labelled precedent, not prediction — it cannot
  establish when Belgrade or any single country will announce.

The architecture matters more than the page. `src/data/national-days.json` is the
**single master**. `ListingPage` now GENERATES the National Day row on a profile
from it, and the duplicated fact row has been deleted from `turkiye.json`. A date
held in two places drifts apart — that is precisely the failure that produced the
four contradicting profiles repaired in the previous commit, and it is designed
out here rather than promised against. Profiles with a documented date also get a
tile back to the record; profiles without one get nothing, which is verified in
the build (Germany renders no such link).

The page reaches ~1,500 words without padding. That was not a target — it is
what saying the true thing carefully costs, once you explain what a National Day
is, why its date is not derivable, what the announcement does not establish, and
how the list is maintained.
