## Are Expo 2027 tickets on sale yet?

No. As of **17 September 2026**, Expo 2027 Belgrade tickets are **not on sale** and no prices have been announced. The official Expo 2027 programme page still displays the status **"Tickets available soon"** in English and **"Ulaznice uskoro dostupne"** in Serbian — the same status it carried a month ago.

The significant thing is what did *not* happen. The organiser's own timetable put the sales start on **15 September 2026**. That day came and went with no launch, no price list, and no announcement of a delay. Serbian outlets cover Expo 2027 almost daily and ran a steady stream of Expo news across 15–17 September — Jamaica signing its participation agreement, a technical-cooperation deal, Slovak travel packages — with nothing about tickets going on sale.

## Two start dates have now slipped

This is the second missed date, not the first, and that pattern is itself the most useful thing to know when you are trying to plan a trip:

| Date | Where it came from | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 May 2026 | Finance Minister Siniša Mali, at a government session on 25 January 2026: "on 15 May we start selling tickets in Serbia and around the world" | Passed. No sales, no follow-up announcement. |
| 15 September 2026 | The organiser's ticket-printing tender: the first batch had to be delivered by 14 September "so that sales can begin on 15 September," reported by Serbian business media on 6 August 2026 | Passed. No sales, no announcement, status unchanged. |

The two dates carry different weight, and it is worth separating them. The first was a public commitment by a minister, with a symbolic date attached — exactly one year before the Expo opens. The second was never a public on-sale announcement at all: it was a delivery deadline inside a procurement document, which is why this page called it *planned* rather than confirmed. Neither is operative now.

We cannot tell you *why* the dates slipped, because the organiser has not said. What we can tell you is that no third date has been given, and that a ticketing schedule slipping this far out says nothing in itself about whether the Expo is on time — participation agreements, pavilion contracts and construction have kept moving throughout.

## The one date that still stands: 1 November 2026, in Slovakia

On **17 September 2026**, at a "Visit Serbia" business meeting in Bratislava, Slovak tourism representatives announced that **Slovak travel agencies will sell packages that include an Expo 2027 ticket** — bundled with accommodation, transport and tours of other parts of Serbia — and that the offer will be available to Slovak citizens **from 1 November 2026**. Igor Kovačević, executive director of Expo 2027 d.o.o. Belgrade, took part in the meeting; Slovakia was the first country to sign a participation agreement for the Expo.

Two things follow from that, and one does not:

- **A real inventory date exists again.** For an agency to sell a package containing an Expo ticket from 1 November, the organiser has to be able to supply tickets, and prices, to the travel trade by then. That is an inference rather than a statement — but it is the firmest timing signal currently available, and firmer than the two dates that slipped, because a commercial partner has published it.
- **National travel-trade partners are a sales channel.** Alongside the physical box offices implied by the printing contract, packages sold through a participating country's agencies are now a confirmed route to a ticket.
- **It is not a general on-sale date.** It applies to packages sold by Slovak agencies to Slovak customers. No price was published, and no equivalent arrangement has been announced for any other country. If you are not buying through a Slovak agency, 1 November 2026 does not yet give you a way to buy a ticket.

## What the August 2026 printing contract tells us

On **18 August 2026** the company EXPO 2027 awarded the contract for producing and printing non-personalised tickets to the **Institute for Manufacturing Banknotes and Coins – Topčider** (Zavod za izradu novčanica i kovanog novca), the Serbian state security printer, for **38.2 million dinars excluding VAT**.

The quantities are the useful part:

| Item | Quantity |
| --- | --- |
| One-day tickets | 1,000,000 |
| Three-day tickets | 200,000 |
| Blank forms | 800,000 |

Three things follow from that, and they are worth separating from speculation:

- **There is a one-day ticket and a three-day ticket.** This is the first official confirmation of any Expo 2027 ticket category. It does not rule out other types — a season pass, concessions, group tickets — but those two now exist as products.
- **Physical tickets are planned, for physical sale.** The contract describes tickets intended for sale at physical points of sale and box offices, printed on protected paper with watermarks, holographic foil, QR codes, perforation, serial numbering and UV-visible security elements. That is banknote-grade anti-counterfeiting, and it implies counter sales, not a purely app-based entry.
- **Printed stock looks like a minority channel — but that is an inference, not a fact.** The 1.2 million printed tickets sit against official projections of 4 million visits and, more recently, more than 6 million. Be careful with the arithmetic, though: 200,000 of those are three-day tickets, so the printed stock covers more admissions than it has pieces, and nothing stops the organiser printing more. It is reasonable to expect a substantial digital channel; it is not established that most admissions will be digital.

The same tender set the delivery schedule that produced the 15 September date: a first batch of 160,000 one-day and 40,000 three-day tickets by 14 September 2026, a second batch by 30 September, the remainder by 31 December 2026. Whether that first batch was actually delivered has not been reported, so it is not known whether the sales start slipped because of printing or for some other reason.

What the contract still does **not** tell you: prices, the online sales channel, concession categories, or whether a season pass exists. Those remain unannounced.

## How much do Expo 2027 tickets cost?

Expo 2027 ticket prices are **not yet announced** as of 17 September 2026. The organiser has not published any per-day, multi-day, or season pass pricing, so **any specific figure you see quoted elsewhere is not an official Expo price.** If a website states an Expo 2027 ticket price as fact today, treat it with caution — there isn't one yet.

For context on what to *expect* (clearly: expected, not confirmed), Specialised-Expo tickets at recent editions have typically been sold as a tiered range — single-day adult tickets, discounted child/student/senior tickets, and multi-day or season passes — usually cheaper if bought online in advance than at the gate. Belgrade's organiser has not confirmed any of this structure, but it's the normal pattern and gives you a sense of how pricing will likely be framed when it appears.

## Where will Expo 2027 tickets be sold?

The **[official Expo 2027 website](https://expobelgrade2027.org/en/intro) (expobelgrade2027.org)** is the primary channel for ticket information, and its programme page is where the "Tickets available soon" status appears. When prices and a sales mechanism are announced, this is where they will show up first.

Beyond that, two channels are now visible:

- **Physical box offices.** The August 2026 printing contract describes tickets produced for sale at physical points of sale and box offices, so counter sales are clearly planned. Where those box offices will be has not been said.
- **National travel agencies, as packages.** From 1 November 2026, Slovak agencies are due to sell bundles containing an Expo ticket (see above). This is the first named third-party channel of any kind, and a model other participating countries may copy.

What remains unconfirmed is everything else — whether there is a dedicated app, general authorised resellers, or direct online sales from the official site. For most major Expos the official website and app are the main sales points, often supplemented by authorised resellers, but for Belgrade this is expected, not confirmed. **Buy only through channels the official site explicitly names** once they're published, to avoid unofficial resellers.

## When do Expo 2027 tickets go on sale?

**No date currently stands for the general public.** The 15 September 2026 plan passed without a launch, the ministerial 15 May 2026 date passed before it, and the organiser has not offered a replacement. The honest answer today is that nobody outside the organiser knows.

Three things would each be a genuine signal, and we watch all three:

- A price or a sales page appearing at expobelgrade2027.org, replacing the "Tickets available soon" status.
- The 1 November 2026 Slovak package sales actually opening — which would mean ticket inventory and pricing exist, even if a general on-sale has not been announced.
- Serbian procurement notices for a ticketing platform or online sales system, the kind of contract that has repeatedly surfaced Expo plans before the organiser announced them.

We update this page on the day any of them lands.

## What is not yet announced

Several things remain open as of September 2026:

- **Prices.** No official per-day, multi-day, or season pass pricing has been published.
- **A sales-start date.** Two planned dates have passed and no third has been given.
- **Concessions and further categories.** The one-day and three-day types are confirmed, but there is no official information on concessions — child, student, senior, disability — on group or bundled tickets, or on whether a season pass exists.
- **Reservation and entry systems.** No official information has been released on time-slot reservations, daily capacity caps, or online queueing and booking mechanisms for general visitors.
- **The online channel.** Physical box-office sales are implied by the printing contract and travel-trade packages are confirmed for Slovakia, but direct online sales have not been described.

If you find a price stated as fact today, treat it with caution: as of 17 September 2026, there isn't an official one.

## Will there be time-slot reservations or capacity limits?

No official information on reservation or entry systems — time-slot reservations, daily capacity caps, or online booking and queueing for general visitors — has been published as of September 2026.

It is reasonable to *expect* some form of capacity management, because the numbers are large and getting larger. The official Participants FAQ models around 4.1 million visits over the 93 days, averaging about 44,652 per day with peaks up to roughly 89,304. The BIE now states more than 6 million, which on the same 93 days would average closer to 64,500 a day. These are projections, not counts, and earlier statements ranged from 2.6 million up. We mention them only because numbers like these are the kind of context that often justifies time-slot or capacity-based entry systems — but no such system has been confirmed for Expo 2027.

## Grounded context while you wait

A few things are confirmed and worth knowing as you plan. Expo 2027 runs from **15 May to 15 August 2027 — 93 days — at the Surčin site** in Belgrade, roughly 5 km from [Nikola Tesla Airport](/glossary/nikola-tesla-airport) and about 13.5 km from downtown. That window is the period any eventual ticket would cover, so it's the dates to build a trip around even before tickets exist.

A ticket delay is not a reason to leave the rest of the trip late. Belgrade's bed supply is the real constraint on an Expo visit — the city has far fewer rooms than a 93-day event of this size normally has behind it — and accommodation can be booked now, while tickets cannot.

Some independent visitor-guide websites, explicitly unaffiliated with the organisers, already publish estimated trip costs such as airport buses, taxis, and city passes. These are not official Expo or government figures, are not ticket prices, and can change quickly. They may help with budgeting your wider trip, but they tell you nothing about Expo entry.
