## Where is Expo 2027 located?

Expo 2027 is in the municipality of Surčin, in the western part of Belgrade, southwest of the city centre — and that location is the single most useful fact for planning your trip. The official Expo planning figures put the site approximately 13.5 kilometres from the city centre and just 5 kilometres from Nikola Tesla International Airport.

One main contractor describes the site as "about 15 km from the center of Belgrade" — that is a rounded estimate, while 13.5 km is the official planning value; both agree on the 5 km to the airport. The secure venue covers 25 hectares, and the wider footprint, including the Expo village, services and parking, reaches 113 hectares. (Serbian spatial-planning documents describe an even larger 167-hectare zone that takes in the adjacent National Stadium; that figure refers to the whole development area, not the Expo grounds you will visit.)

Because the grounds sit out by the airport rather than in the old town, almost every practical question about getting to Expo 2027 comes down to one thing: how the local transport network connects the airport, the centre and Surčin by the time the gates open in May 2027.

## How far is Expo 2027 from the airport?

About 5 km. The official Expo documents place the Surčin site roughly 5 kilometres from Nikola Tesla International Airport — meaning that if you fly in, you land closer to the Expo grounds than to central Belgrade (around 13.5 km away).

That proximity is the planning win to remember. The new railway now under construction runs right past the airport on its way to Surčin and the National Stadium, and a dedicated Expo bus line from the airport, EKSPO 1, is planned from 1 April 2027. Until then, the dependable way to cover those 5 km is by road, in a taxi or a ride-hail car (see below), because the railway has no timetable yet.

## Flying to Belgrade for Expo 2027

Most international visitors will arrive at Belgrade Nikola Tesla Airport (BEG), about 5 km from the Expo site. **[Air Serbia](aff:go/air-serbia)**, the national carrier, has its hub here and the widest direct network into Belgrade. Wikipedia's destinations list (as of August 2026) puts its Belgrade network at 89 destinations in 35 countries; treat that count as approximate. For readers in the German-speaking countries, Belgrade Airport's seasonal timetable (checked October 2026) shows Air Serbia flying to Frankfurt, Munich, Berlin, Düsseldorf, Hamburg, Stuttgart, Nuremberg and Hanover in Germany, Vienna and Salzburg (seasonal, weekly) in Austria, and Zürich and Geneva in Switzerland. Cologne/Bonn is served by Eurowings rather than Air Serbia, and Lufthansa and Austrian Airlines also fly from Frankfurt and Vienna. The timetable has no Bratislava flight at all, so the nearest option from Slovakia is Vienna. Routes and frequencies change by season, so **check the current schedule on the airline's site** before you plan around any of them.

Low-cost carriers also serve Belgrade from several European cities, so compare fares across airlines rather than assuming one is cheapest. The Expo runs from 15 May to 15 August 2027. We expect flights and hotels to be busiest on opening weekend and in peak summer weeks, though nobody has published figures. Book flights and rooms once your dates are firm, ideally on changeable fares (see [where to stay for Expo 2027](/expo-2027/where-to-stay-for-expo)).

**Arriving:** because the site is only about 5 km from the airport, a taxi or ride-hail car to Surčin is a short trip. The Expo airport bus line, EKSPO 1, is planned from 1 April 2027. For groups, business visitors on a fixed schedule, or anyone landing late with luggage, a [pre-booked private transfer](aff:go/airport-transfer) (driver waiting in arrivals, fixed price paid in advance) is worth comparing with the voucher taxi; it usually costs more. Our [airport-to-city guide](/plan-your-trip/airport-to-city#when-is-a-pre-booked-transfer-worth-it) sets out when it is worth it.

## How do I get from the airport into the city today?

As of 2026, all regular Belgrade city public transport is free. Since 1 January 2025, buses (including line 72 and its night variant 72N), trams, trolleybuses and the [BG Voz](/glossary/bg-voz) suburban trains carry no tickets, fares or validation on regular city lines. This is a real, current policy — but it is also a perishable one. A future administration could change it, so it is worth re-checking closer to your travel date.

For reaching the centre, city bus line 72 connects the central Zeleni venac terminal with [Nikola Tesla Airport](/glossary/nikola-tesla-airport), and the night variant 72N runs between Republic Square and the airport. Buses leave roughly every half hour and the ride takes about 40 minutes. As regular city lines, both are free to ride. Older guides often described bus 72 as the cheapest way into town; under the current policy it simply costs nothing. Other lines also serve the airport area — among them 600 (central railway station–airport) and 607 (Banovo Brdo–airport) — all free as regular city services. All airport buses depart from stops just outside the Arrivals hall on the ground floor.

If you have read a guidebook that tells you to buy a ticket or a travel card, that advice predates the change. Belgrade used to run a card-and-ticket system — [BusPlus](/glossary/busplus) from 2012, then Beograd Plus from May 2023 — with tickets bought at kiosks, by app or by SMS. Since transport became free, there are no fares to pay on regular lines, and the app now serves route and schedule information rather than ticketing. (Old single, day and weekly ticket prices you may still see quoted online no longer apply.)

The one paid exception is the A1 airport minibus, which runs between Nikola Tesla Airport and Trg Slavija, stopping in New Belgrade (Fontana). It is a separate paid shuttle. It is reported to run roughly every 15 to 30 minutes during the day, with an on-board ticket costing about 400 RSD in cash and a journey of about 30 to 40 minutes to Slavija. Treat that fare as indicative for 2026 — it has risen over time and may change by 2027.

For taxis, the airport operates an official voucher system: you take a fixed-price voucher from an e-kiosk, then go to the taxi stand, and the driver charges only the fare on the voucher. An official voucher taxi costs 3,500 RSD to the central districts (Stari grad, Vračar, Savski venac) and 2,700 RSD to New Belgrade, Zemun and Surčin, at the City of Belgrade's zone fares as reported since August 2025, while an app-based [CarGo](/glossary/cargo) ride runs around 1,500 to 2,500 RSD, with the drive taking about 25 minutes. Belgrade has no Uber or Bolt; the main ride-hail apps are Yandex Go and CarGo. The driving distance to the centre (Trg Republike) is about 18 km via the E-70, around 20 to 25 minutes by car. These are indicative 2026 figures. For more on these options, see our guide to the [airport to city](/plan-your-trip/airport-to-city) journey.

## What's the best way to get to Expo from central Belgrade?

As of 2026, the most reliable option is Belgrade's regular city public transport — currently free on all regular lines (buses, trams, trolleybuses and the BG Voz suburban trains). For door-to-door convenience to Surčin, a taxi or a ride-hail app (CarGo or Yandex Go) covers the roughly 13.5 km from the centre directly to the site.

Two purpose-built options are on the way. Four Expo bus lines from different parts of the city were announced to start on 1 November 2026 (see [the Expo bus lines](#the-expo-bus-lines-what-has-been-announced) below), and a new railway from Beograd Centar (Prokop) to the National Stadium next to the site is expected in spring 2027. Until both are confirmed running, plan on the free network plus a ride-hail car, and check the Expo lines closer to your travel date.

## The new railway to Surčin: what's confirmed and what's still a promise

A new double-track, electrified railway is under construction from Zemun Polje to the National Stadium, passing Nikola Tesla Airport and Surčin. It runs 18.3 km, with stops at Singidunum, the airport and Surčin, and a station at the National Stadium. The main contractor is Powerchina International Group, working for the Government of Serbia, with construction having begun in 2024. As of early 2026, the project's first phase covers the Zemun Polje–National Stadium section (a later phase is planned to extend the line toward Obrenovac), with reporting describing more than 500 people, including around 200 engineers, working on site and major bridges, a viaduct and an overpass under construction along the route.

Completion timing is where caution matters most. Serbian officials, including the President and the Minister of Construction, have stated the line is targeted for the end of 2026, with one statement giving a date of 1 December 2026, after which a testing phase begins (reported to start around February 2027). More recent reporting describes the railway entering service in spring 2027. These are officially stated targets, reported as provisional — not officially confirmed and not a binding timetable. Officials have promised journeys of about 15 minutes between the airport and central Belgrade (Prokop), with trains every 30 minutes and up to every 15 at peak. Latest update (October 2026): Finance Minister Siniša Mali said construction should finish by the end of 2026, with about 11 of the 18.3 km complete, and that the line should open in March or April 2027 after testing — a ministerial projection, not a timetable.

What is still unknown: no detailed passenger timetable, exact opening date, or fare scheme for this railway during the Expo period has yet been announced. With regular transport currently free, whether this rail link will be free or carry a special fare is itself not yet specified. In short, the line is a real, advanced construction project on an officially stated 2026–2027 timeline — but do not build your travel plans on it being open until an actual opening date and timetable are published.

## How will visitors reach the site during the event?

According to the Participants FAQ, visitors will reach the site by private vehicle, public bus, urban rail and river transport, with on-site parking for up to 11,000 vehicles and charging for e-bikes and scooters. The Participation Guide adds that an urban railway will connect the site to the transport network, public buses will serve entrances A and C, and new environmentally friendly bus lines will link the site with the airport, downtown and surrounding municipalities — though it notes this is preliminary and subject to update.

Serbia is procuring electric buses for the event, with charging infrastructure at the Expo complex, the airport and major terminals; after the event they are reported to pass to GSP Belgrade for regular service. The fleet size is contested: early statements described 100 electric buses, while a later tender and contract secured 50 plus 8 chargers. At least 50 are now contractually committed; the final deployed total may differ.

## The Expo bus lines: what has been announced

On 10 October 2026, EXPO 2027 d.o.o. announced direct city bus lines to the exhibition complex in Surčin, as reported by Biznis.rs, Kurir and B92:

| Line | Starts at | Announced start |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EKSPO 2 | Belgrade Waterfront (Beograd na vodi) loop, under the Gazela bridge, Savski venac | 1 November 2026 |
| EKSPO 3 | Zemun (Bačka street) | 1 November 2026 |
| EKSPO 4 | Vukov spomenik, Zvezdara, via the Terazije tunnel, Zeleni venac and Branko's Bridge | 1 November 2026 |
| EKSPO 5 | Ustanička, Zvezdara | 1 November 2026 |
| EKSPO 1 | Nikola Tesla Airport, via Surčin | 1 April 2027 |
| EKSPO 6 | Belvil, near Delta City, New Belgrade | 1 May 2027 |
| EKSPO 7 | Block 45, New Belgrade | 1 May 2027 |

What the announcement says beyond that:

- **Frequency:** for the first four lines, buses every 15–22 minutes for most of the day, with 31 eco-buses until the end of March 2027. More frequent service is planned from 1 May 2027, when more than 90 buses are to run.
- **More lines from May 2027:** three local feeder lines, and a bus from the railway's National Stadium station to the Expo entrance.

What is still open, as of 16 November 2026:

- **Whether the first four lines are running.** All the reporting we found predates the 1 November start, and the city transport operator had published nothing on them.
- **Their stops and exact timetables.** Only the starting points and main roads were published.
- **Whether they are free** like regular city transport. The organiser has not said.

Check the line you need before relying on it. Our [where to stay for Expo 2027](/expo-2027/where-to-stay-for-expo) page compares each part of the city by which of these lines and railway stations serve it.

## What about the metro and BG Voz?

The Belgrade Metro is under construction, but officials have targeted Line 1 for around 2028 and Line 2 around 2030 — both after the Expo window, and stated as political dates rather than contractual ones. Crucially, no planned metro line reaches Surčin, the airport or the Expo site. Rail access is intended via BG Voz suburban rail instead. A planned BG Voz line (Line 4), 16.2 km, is meant to run Novi Beograd–Nikola Tesla Airport–Surčin–National Stadium, but it is a planned future line, not a service running today — don't expect to board it.

For now, the practical advice is to treat the airport and the free regular network as your reliable backbone, with a taxi or ride-hail car to cover the last stretch to Surčin door-to-door, and to check the Expo bus lines and the railway as their details are confirmed. For the wider picture on moving around the city, see [getting around Belgrade](/plan-your-trip/getting-around).
