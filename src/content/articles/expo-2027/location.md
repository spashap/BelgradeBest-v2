## Where exactly is Expo 2027?

Expo 2027 Belgrade is being built in **Surčin**, the city's westernmost municipality, on the flat farmland of the Srem plain between New Belgrade, Nikola Tesla Airport and the Sava. The organiser calls it a "brand-new urban zone" rather than a venue inside the existing city, and that is the key point for planning: **the Expo is not in central Belgrade**. The old town, Knez Mihailova and the riverfront are a transport ride away, not a walk.

The site sits close to the **Miloš Veliki motorway** near the Surčin exit and the Belgrade bypass, which is how most road traffic will arrive. Its immediate neighbours are the new **National Stadium**, the Expo **residential zone** (the athletes'-village-style housing for staff and participants) and, about 5 km to the north-east, **Nikola Tesla Airport**.

## Is there an address or an official map?

Not yet. As of 15 November 2026 the organiser has published **no street or postal address, no GPS point for the gates and no visitor map**, and the list of entrances has not been released either. Any precise pin you see on a third-party site is an estimate.

Until the official map appears, the safest description is "the Expo 2027 site in Surčin, next to the National Stadium". We will add the address, the map link and the entrances here as soon as the organiser publishes them on [expobelgrade2027.org](https://www.expobelgrade2027.org).

## How far is it from the centre and the airport?

| From | Distance | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| City centre (Republic Square) | ~13.5 km | Straight line, organiser's figure; one official article says 13 km |
| Nikola Tesla Airport | ~5 km | The Expo's nearest neighbour |
| Belgrade Centre (Prokop) station | ~32 min by train (planned) | Once the new railway opens, expected spring 2027 |

Two cautions. First, **13.5 km is a straight-line figure**: the road distance is longer, and some guides round it to 15 km. Second, drive times depend on rush-hour traffic on the motorway and the bridges over the Sava, so treat any "20 minutes by car" claim as a best case. For routes, fares and taxi tips, see [getting to Expo 2027](/expo-2027/getting-there).

## How will you get there?

Three things are planned specifically for the Expo:

- **A new railway.** An electrified, double-track line of about 18 km branches off at Zemun Polje and runs via Singidunum, the airport and Surčin to a **National Stadium** station beside the site. Trains from Belgrade Centre (Prokop) are planned to take about 32 minutes. In October 2026 Finance Minister Siniša Mali said construction should finish by the end of 2026 and the line should open in **March or April 2027** — a projection, not a timetable.
- **Dedicated EKSPO bus lines.** Serbian business media reported in October 2026 that four direct lines (EKSPO 2–5, from the Belgrade Waterfront terminus, Zemun and two Zvezdara termini) were to start on **1 November 2026**, with a full network of seven direct lines from 1 May 2027. We have not been able to confirm that the November lines are running, so check before relying on them.
- **Parking.** The organiser counts 9,000 parking spaces in the new district; their location and pricing have not been published.

Public transport inside Belgrade has been free since 1 January 2025, and the regular city buses to Surčin keep running alongside the Expo services.

## What is around the site?

The Expo district is surrounded by suburban and rural Belgrade rather than tourist neighbourhoods:

- **Surčin** itself is a small town and municipality of about 45,000 people (2022 census), with villages such as Jakovo, Bečmen, Boljevci and Dobanovci. It has few hotels and almost no visitor infrastructure yet.
- **New Belgrade** (Novi Beograd), to the north-east, is the nearest big district with [business hotels](aff:area/new-belgrade), shopping and restaurants — the usual compromise between the Expo and the centre.
- **Zemun** and the **old town** across the Sava have the atmosphere, but a longer daily commute.

For a side-by-side of these options, see [where to stay for Expo 2027](/expo-2027/where-to-stay-for-expo).

## How big is it?

The **exhibition grounds cover 25 hectares**. They sit inside a larger planned complex of about **113 hectares** that also holds the National Stadium, an aquatic centre and the Expo residential zone; the wider construction programme around it is larger still, so you will see much bigger hectare figures quoted for different boundaries. The organiser says the project adds **10 km of new boulevards**.

For scale, Expo 2027 is a **Specialised Expo** — shorter and smaller than a World Expo such as Dubai 2020 or Osaka 2025 — so a full visit is a day or two, not a week. See [what kind of Expo this is](/expo-2027/what-is-it).

## What will the site become after the Expo?

The plan is that **the whole site stays in use** after 15 August 2027:

- a new **Belgrade fairground** and a **museum district** with an auditorium and an innovation centre;
- **schools, kindergartens and sports halls** built from reused modular pavilions;
- about **1,500 flats** from the residential zone, rented during the Expo and sold or let afterwards;
- the **National Stadium** (about 52,000 seats) and the **aquatic centre**, both permanent.

President Aleksandar Vučić has said the city will grow towards Surčin around the new district. That makes the Expo less a temporary fairground than the first piece of a new part of Belgrade — worth knowing if you are visiting on business.
