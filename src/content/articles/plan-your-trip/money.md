## What currency does Belgrade use?

Belgrade, like the rest of Serbia, uses the **[Serbian dinar](/glossary/dinar)** (code RSD). Price tags and menus show it as "RSD", "din." or "дин." Notes come in 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1,000, 2,000 and 5,000 dinars, and coins in 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20 dinars.

**The euro rate barely moves.** The official National Bank of Serbia rate stayed between about 117.0 and 117.3 dinars to the euro through 2025, and it was 117.62 on 13 November 2026. So a working conversion is:

- **€1 ≈ 117.6 RSD**
- **100 RSD ≈ €0.85**
- **1,000 RSD ≈ €8.50**

Against the US dollar and the pound the dinar moves more (about 105 dinars to the dollar in mid-November 2026), so check those on the day.

Prices are written the Serbian way, with a dot for thousands and a comma for decimals: "1.200,00 din." is one thousand two hundred dinars, about €10.

## Can you pay in euros in Belgrade?

**No, not for everyday spending.** Serbia's Law on Foreign Exchange Operations (Article 34) says payments inside the country are made in dinars, with a few narrow exceptions. The same article allows a price to be *agreed* in euros as long as it is *paid* in dinars. That is why you see euro prices but pay in dinars. In practice:

- **Shops, cafés, restaurants, taxis, museums and the airport minibus take dinars.** The A1 minibus, for example, accepts only dinar cash.
- **Hotels, apartments, tour operators and car-hire firms often quote in euros.** You pay the dinar equivalent. If the card terminal offers to charge you in euros or your home currency, choose dinars, and if the bill doesn't show the rate used, ask.
- **Some tourist-facing places take euro notes anyway**, usually at a rate that favours them. You can't insist on paying that way, and you lose on the conversion.
- **Bring euros to exchange, not to spend.** The euro is the easiest foreign currency to change in Belgrade.

## Cash or card in Belgrade?

**Mostly card.** Visa and Mastercard are accepted in most hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, larger shops and malls, usually contactless. A phone wallet works wherever the terminal takes contactless cards. American Express is accepted much less often. Dinar cash still matters at the small end:

| Paying for | Card | Cash (dinars) |
|---|---|---|
| Hotels, most restaurants, supermarkets, malls | Yes, usually contactless | Yes |
| City buses, trams and trolleybuses | Free for everyone since 1 January 2025 | Free |
| A1 airport minibus | No | Yes, to the driver |
| Airport voucher taxi | Not guaranteed | Carry the fare |
| Ride-hail apps (CarGo, Yandex Go) | In the app | Some apps allow it |
| Street taxis | Some cabs; ask first | Safest |
| Bakeries, kiosks, green markets, small kafanas | Often not | Yes |
| Nikola Tesla Museum | No | Yes |
| Tips | Rarely added to a card payment | Yes |

You don't need to carry much. A few thousand dinars covers the minibus, a bakery breakfast, a market stall and tips, and ATMs make topping up easy.

## ATMs: fees and the "pay in your own currency" trap

ATMs are on almost every central street and pay out dinars. Watch for two costs.

**1. The conversion offer.** Many ATMs and card terminals offer to charge you in your home currency at a "guaranteed" rate. This is dynamic currency conversion (DCC), and its rate includes a markup over your bank's. **Choose dinars (RSD), or "without conversion", every time**, and let your own bank do the converting. The same applies when a waiter brings a card terminal to the table.

**2. The machine's own fee.** Many Serbian bank ATMs charge foreign cards a fee for each withdrawal. It is commonly reported at **400–600 RSD (about €3.40–5.10)**. The screen shows the fee before you confirm, so you can cancel and try another bank's machine. Your own bank may charge as well.

Fewer, larger withdrawals keep per-withdrawal fees down, but balance that against carrying a lot of cash. Machines on the wall of a bank branch are the safest choice. For skimming devices, "helpers" at the machine and other tricks, see [staying safe in Belgrade](/plan-your-trip/staying-safe).

## Where to exchange money in Belgrade

The best place to change cash is a licensed exchange office, a **[menjačnica](/glossary/menjacnica)**. There is one on almost every central street, with its rates on a board outside.

- **Euro rates have a legal limit.** National Bank rules keep an exchange office's euro buying and selling rates within 1.25% of the official middle rate; the bank reminded offices of this in October 2026. At mid-November's rate, that means you should get at least about 116 dinars for each euro you sell.
- **For other currencies, compare two boards** before changing a large sum. The rule we could confirm covers the euro.
- **Check the board for a commission** (*provizija*) before you hand over cash. Count your money at the counter and keep the receipt.
- **Bring clean notes.** Torn or marked notes are sometimes refused.
- **Euros are the easiest currency to change.** Boards also list other major currencies, but a less common one, such as the Philippine peso, may not be taken. Ask first, or use an ATM.
- **Change only small amounts at the airport, stations and malls**, where rates are often less favourable than in town.

## Getting dinars when you land

At Nikola Tesla Airport you can withdraw dinars from the ATMs in the baggage-claim area and the international arrivals hall, or change cash at the 24-hour exchange offices and machines. How much you need depends on how you get into town:

- **Bus 72:** free, so you need nothing.
- **A1 minibus:** about 400 RSD, in cash, to the driver.
- **Voucher taxi:** 2,700 RSD to New Belgrade and Zemun, 3,500 RSD to the old town. Carry the fare in dinars.
- **Ride-hail app:** usually paid by card in the app.
- **Pre-booked transfer:** paid by card when you book, so you need no cash on arrival.

The airport guide compares all five, [with prices and how to pay for each](/plan-your-trip/airport-to-city).

## Paying for your stay

- **Euro prices, dinar payments.** Booking sites and hotels often show prices in euros. If you pay at the property, you pay the dinar equivalent; if the card terminal offers your home currency, choose dinars.
- **Tourist tax.** Belgrade charges 240 RSD per adult per night (since 15 October 2026, up from 160 RSD), with exemptions and reductions for children. The property collects it, and it isn't always in the price you see online, so ask.
- **Apartments.** Keep payment on the booking platform. A host who presses you to pay outside it is a warning sign. More in [hotels vs apartments](/where-to-stay/belgrade-hotels-vs-apartments).

Still choosing an area? [Where to stay in Belgrade](/where-to-stay) compares the neighbourhoods.

## Tipping in Belgrade

Tipping is appreciated, not required, and bills don't usually include a service charge.

- **Cafés and casual meals:** round up.
- **Sit-down restaurants and kafanas:** about 10% for good service. Kafana musicians are tipped separately and directly.
- **Taxis:** round up the fare.
- **Hotels:** a small tip for a porter or housekeeping if you wish.
- **Fast food and bakeries:** no tip.

Tip in cash. Many card terminals can't add a tip, so keep a few small notes back. More on paying in restaurants is in [how eating and drinking works in Belgrade](/food-and-nightlife/eating-and-drinking-in-belgrade).

## Is Belgrade expensive?

For day-to-day spending, it costs less than most Western European capitals. City buses, trams and trolleybuses are free for everyone, and eating out is good value. For current benchmarks on coffee, a grill meal and a kafana dinner, see [what eating and drinking costs](/food-and-nightlife/eating-and-drinking-in-belgrade). Rooms vary most, by area and by season. ## Leaving Serbia: leftover dinars, cash limits and VAT refunds

- **Change leftover dinars before you go**, at an exchange office in town. Dinars are hard to change outside Serbia.
- **Large amounts of cash.** If you bring in more than €10,000 in foreign cash (or the same value in another currency), declare it to customs when you arrive and keep the confirmation they give you. You need it to take the money out again.
- **VAT refund.** If you don't live in Serbia, you can claim back VAT on goods you take home in your luggage once you spend at least 6,000 RSD, VAT included, at one seller.
  - Ask the shop for the refund form, paper or electronic, when you pay.
  - Show the goods, receipts and form to customs when you leave, and they stamp it.
  - The goods must leave Serbia by the end of the third month after the month you bought them.
  - The refund is paid in dinars, by the shop or a refund operator, minus their fee.
