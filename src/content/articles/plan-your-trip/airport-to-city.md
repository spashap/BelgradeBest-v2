## What's the best way from Belgrade airport to the city centre?

The short answer:

- **Free:** take **bus 72** to Zeleni venac in the centre.
- **Fast and cheap with luggage room:** take the paid **A1 minibus** to Slavija, about 400 RSD in cash.
- **Door to door:** use the airport's **official voucher taxi** (a fixed fare by zone, 3,500 RSD to the old town) or a **ride-hail app** such as Yandex Go or [CarGo](/glossary/cargo). There is no Uber or Bolt in Belgrade.

A direct airport–city train is being built but is not running yet.

| Option | Goes to | Time | Price (Nov 2026) | How you pay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bus 72 | Zeleni venac (old town) | 30–40 min | Free | Nothing to pay, no ticket |
| Bus 600 | Beograd Centar (Prokop) railway station | 30–40 min | Free | Nothing to pay, no ticket |
| Bus 72N (night) | Republic Square | 30–40 min | Free | Nothing to pay, no ticket |
| A1 minibus | Slavija Square | about 30 min | About 400 RSD | Cash in dinars, to the driver |
| Voucher taxi | Your door | 20–40 min | 3,500 RSD to the old town, Vračar and Savski venac; 2,700 RSD to New Belgrade and Zemun | To the driver, the amount on the voucher |
| Ride-hail app | Your door | 20–40 min | Shown in the app before you book | In the app (card) or cash, depending on the app |
| Pre-booked transfer | Your door | 20–40 min | About €34 to Republic Square (one quote, Oct 2026) | By card when you book |

Bus journey times are the airport's own figures. Fares and timetables change, so treat every price here as dated November 2026 and check again close to travel.

## How do you pay? Cash, cards and apps

- **City buses (72, 72N, 600, 607):** nothing to pay. Belgrade's regular public transport has been free for everyone since 1 January 2025, so there is no ticket to buy or validate.
- **A1 minibus:** you buy the ticket from the driver, and the airport says payment is **only in cash, in dinars (RSD)**. Euros and cards are not accepted, so have dinars before you get on.
- **Voucher taxi:** the kiosk prints the fare, and you pay the driver that amount at the end of the ride. We could not confirm that every voucher taxi takes cards, so carry the fare in dinars.
- **Ride-hail apps:** the price is set in the app before you confirm. Card payment in the app is the norm, and some apps also allow cash.
- **Pre-booked transfer:** paid by card when you book, so nothing changes hands at the airport.

**Getting dinars at the airport.** The arrivals area has two banks of ATMs (in the baggage-claim area and in the international arrivals hall), exchange offices open around the clock, and 24-hour currency-exchange machines. Airport exchange rates are usually worse than in town. Take out or change only what you need for the ride, and decline the "pay in your own currency" offer at the ATM. Our money guide explains [where to get dinars and the airport exchange trap](/plan-your-trip/money).

## First, the basics

[Nikola Tesla Airport](/glossary/nikola-tesla-airport) (BEG) is about 18 km from the city centre by road, via the E-70 motorway and the Gazela bridge. A drive takes 20–40 minutes depending on traffic.

- Republic Square and [Knez Mihailova](/glossary/knez-mihailova), the old-town core, are about 18 km away.
- Slavija Square, where the A1 ends, and Zeleni venac, where bus 72 ends, are both central.
- New Belgrade lies between the airport and the old town, which is why its taxi zone is cheaper.

For moving around the city once you arrive, see our guide to [getting around Belgrade](/plan-your-trip/getting-around).

## Is bus 72 to the city centre free, and where does it go?

Yes. Since 1 January 2025 all regular Belgrade city public transport has been free for every passenger, and the airport lists its city lines as free of charge. This was still the policy in November 2026, but a fare policy can change, so check before you rely on it.

The airport is served by four free city lines:

- **Bus 72** runs from the Airport to Zeleni venac, a central terminus just below Knez Mihailova and Republic Square. The stop is outside the terminal and the trip takes 30–40 minutes. It is the workhorse airport bus.
- **Bus 72N** is the night line between Republic Square and the airport, also 30–40 minutes. It is the free option if you land after the day buses stop.
- **Bus 600** runs from the airport to Beograd Centar ("Prokop"), the main railway station, in 30–40 minutes. Take it if you are connecting to a train.
- **Bus 607** runs from the airport to Banovo Brdo, in 30–40 minutes. It suits you only if you are staying in that part of the city.

Bus 72 stops more often than the A1 minibus, through New Belgrade and Surčin, and has no luggage racks. If you are travelling light and want to spend nothing, it is the obvious choice.

You may still see older guides and videos quoting small bus fares (45–150 RSD, for example). Those predate the 2025 free-transport policy and no longer apply.

## What is the A1 minibus, and how much does it cost?

The A1 is a paid minibus between the airport and **Slavija Square (Kralja Milutina Street)**, with few stops. It takes about 30 minutes, so it is quicker into the centre than bus 72. Slavija is a transport hub within walking distance of Vračar, the St. Sava Temple area and many hotels. The A1 is the exception to Belgrade's free public transport: it is a separate service and you pay for it.

- **Price:** about **400 RSD** (a little under €3.50). The airport does not publish the fare, and this is the figure travel sources reported in 2025. Older guides quote 200 RSD, so the price has risen over time.
- **Payment:** buy the ticket from the driver, **in cash, in dinars only**. This is the airport's own wording.
- **Frequency:** departures have been reported every 15–30 minutes in the daytime and less often in the evening. The A1 does not run through the night, so for a late landing see [arriving at night](#arriving-at-night).
- **Stops:** the route has been changed before (a reroute was reported in 2024), so check the board at the stop if you want to get off before Slavija.

## How much is a taxi from Belgrade airport, and how does the voucher work?

The airport runs an official fixed-price taxi system, so you know the fare before you get in:

1. **Get a voucher.** There are four e-kiosks: three in the baggage-claim area and one in the passenger lounge. Choose your destination and the kiosk prints a fare for your zone.
2. **Go to the first taxi in the queue** at the taxi stand in front of the arrivals terminal. Queue-jumping is not allowed.
3. **Pay the driver the amount on the voucher.** The driver must charge only that fare. It covers up to four passengers and luggage that fits in the boot.

The fares are set by the City of Belgrade per zone. These are the figures reported when the kiosks arrived in August 2025, unchanged in reports from January 2026:

| Zone | Main areas | Fare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | New Belgrade, Zemun, Surčin | 2,700 RSD (about €23) |
| 2 | Stari grad (old town), Vračar, Savski venac and nearer parts of Palilula, Voždovac, Zvezdara and Čukarica | 3,500 RSD (about €30) |
| 3–6 | Outer districts, up to Barajevo, Obrenovac, Lazarevac and Mladenovac | 4,300–11,400 RSD |

Some districts are split between zones, so check the price the kiosk shows for your exact address. If a driver asks for more than the voucher says, the city's inspection service takes complaints by email (inspekcija@beograd.gov.rs) or on 11-0-11 from a local phone.

One stable warning: unlicensed taxi touts work the arrivals hall despite the voucher system, and the airport operator has acknowledged it. They approach you before you reach the kiosk and quote inflated flat fares. Use only the kiosk and the marked taxi stand, show the driver your voucher before you set off, and ignore unsolicited offers.

## Is there Uber in Belgrade, or which ride-hail apps work?

Belgrade has no Uber or Bolt. The apps that work are:

- **Yandex Go:** dispatches cars from licensed Belgrade taxi companies and shows an upfront fare. Serbian business press reported that Pink Taxi joined it in February 2026.
- **CarGo:** a local Uber-style platform popular for car quality and transparent pricing. Its regulatory status has been contested over the years, but it keeps operating.
- **Taxi companies' own apps:** dispatch apps such as Pink Taxi and Belgrade Taxi.

App rides can cost less than the voucher taxi and usually let you pay by card. You need mobile data the moment you land to order one. If you are not sure you will have data, sort that out first: [eSIMs, local SIMs and airport wifi](/plan-your-trip/sim-and-connectivity).

## Arriving at night

- **Free:** night bus **72N** runs between the airport and Republic Square. Check its departure times before you fly, because night buses run far less often than day buses.
- **Taxi:** the voucher system works the same at night, at the same zone fares.
- **Cash:** the airport's exchange offices in international arrivals are open 24 hours, as are the currency-exchange machines.
- **Pre-booked:** a [pre-booked transfer](#when-is-a-pre-booked-transfer-worth-it) means a driver is already waiting.
- **Sleeping near the airport:** if you land after midnight and fly on the next day, or would simply rather sleep first, [hotels near the airport](aff:near/airport) save you the drive into town and back.

## From the airport to the train or bus station

- **Trains:** Belgrade's main railway station is **Beograd Centar (Prokop)**, and free **bus 600** goes there directly from the airport. The old central station near the river no longer serves passengers.
- **Intercity buses:** the main bus station (BAS) has been in **Block 42, New Belgrade**, since September 2024. That is the closest of the city's terminals to the airport and in taxi zone 1 (2,700 RSD by voucher), or a short app ride.

Our [stations and onward travel guide](/plan-your-trip/stations-and-onward-travel) covers both stations and trains to Novi Sad.

## When is a pre-booked transfer worth it?

A pre-booked transfer is a private car you reserve before you fly. The driver meets you in the arrivals hall, your flight is tracked so a delay does not leave you without a ride, and the price is fixed and paid in advance. Several companies offer this at Belgrade airport. We checked one of them, [Welcome Pickups](aff:go/airport-transfer), in October 2026: a sedan for one passenger from the airport to Republic Square was quoted at **€34**, taxes included, for a midday arrival. That is a little more than the voucher taxi (3,500 RSD, about €30). It is not the cheapest way into town, and it does not need to be. It earns its price in a few situations:

- **Late-night or very early arrivals**, when you do not want to look for a bus stop or a taxi queue.
- **Families and heavy luggage**: you can book a minivan or minibus instead of a sedan, and request child seats when you book (Welcome Pickups charges extra for them).
- **A first visit**, when you would simply like someone waiting for you and no negotiation at the door.
- **Business trips**, where a fixed price and a punctual driver matter more than saving a few euros.
- **Expo 2027 days**, when you are carrying bags and a schedule. See [getting to Expo 2027](/expo-2027/getting-there).
- **Anyone who wants the price agreed before leaving home**, and wants to avoid the touts described above.

**When it isn't worth it:** if you are travelling alone and light, arriving in daylight, **bus 72 (or the A1 minibus if you want a faster, paid ride) is the better answer.** It costs nothing, or about 400 RSD, and the journey is not hard to work out. A pre-booked transfer is also less flexible than a taxi if your plans change, so read the terms before you pay.

**What the terms look like (Welcome Pickups, checked October 2026):**

- You pay by card when you book; the quoted price is the final price.
- At the airport the driver waits up to **60 minutes free** from your chosen pickup time. If your flight is delayed, the driver tracks it and adjusts. Waiting beyond that costs extra (€6 per 15 minutes in a sedan).
- Cancel **24 hours or more** before pickup and you choose between a coupon for the full amount or 80% back to your card. Cancel later and there is no refund.
- Pick a pickup time about 30 minutes after landing, to allow for passport control and bags.

Prices vary with the date, the vehicle and the address, so get your own quote and compare it with the voucher taxi fare for your zone.

## When will the airport train to the city open?

A new electrified double-track line of about 18.3 km is under construction. It branches from the Belgrade–Novi Sad railway at Zemun Polje and connects the airport with the National Stadium and Expo site at Surčin. The planned [BG Voz](/glossary/bg-voz) stopping pattern is reported as:

Prokop (Beograd Centar) → New Belgrade → Tošin Bunar → Zemun → Altina → Zemun Polje → Singidunum → Belgrade Airport → Surčin → National Stadium

The airport station will be about 500 m from Terminal 1, linked by a footbridge.

- **Opening:** in October 2026 the finance minister put the expected opening at March or April 2027, after testing, in time for the Expo. Earlier reports gave different testing dates, so treat any date as provisional until trains run.
- **Journey time:** about 20 minutes from the centre to the airport, as reported in July 2026.
- **Frequency:** trains every 30 minutes, more often at peak times and during the Expo, as planned.

**What's still unknown:** the fares. It has not been announced whether the link will be free under the city's free-transport policy or carry an airport surcharge. If you are arriving for the Expo, see [getting to Expo 2027](/expo-2027/getting-there) for the latest on connections to the Surčin site.
